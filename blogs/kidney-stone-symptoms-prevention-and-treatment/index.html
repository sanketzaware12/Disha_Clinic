Kidney Stone Symptoms, Prevention and Treatment Options

Learn what kidney stone pain feels like, how stones are treated by size and the simple habits that help prevent them.

ADr. Abhijeet Suresh Shinde8 Nov 20265 min read

Learn what kidney stone pain feels like, how stones are treated by size and the simple habits that help prevent them.

Kidney stones are hard deposits of minerals and salts that form inside the kidney. Some pass without trouble, while others cause severe pain or block the flow of urine. Knowing the symptoms and the treatment choices helps you decide when to wait and when to act.

What are kidney stones?

When urine contains more crystal-forming substances than the fluid can dilute, crystals stick together and grow into stones. A stone may stay in the kidney or move down the ureter, the thin tube that carries urine to the bladder. Size and position decide the symptoms and the treatment.

Common symptoms

Small stones inside the kidney may cause no symptoms at all. A stone that moves into the ureter can cause:

  • Sudden, severe pain in the side or back that may travel to the groin
  • Blood in the urine or pink-coloured urine
  • Burning or frequent urination
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Fever and chills, which can mean an infection

Causes and risk factors

Not drinking enough water is the most common cause. Other factors include a diet high in salt or animal protein, repeated urinary infections, obesity, certain medical conditions and a family history of stones. People who work in hot environments and sweat a lot are also at higher risk.

How kidney stones are diagnosed

Diagnosis usually involves an ultrasound scan, urine tests and sometimes an X-ray or a CT scan to see the exact size and position of the stone. Blood tests check how well the kidneys are working. These details allow the surgeon to choose the safest treatment.

Treatment options

The treatment depends on the size, position and symptoms:

  • Small stones: may pass on their own with plenty of fluids and medicines for pain and for relaxing the ureter, under medical supervision
  • Stones that do not pass or cause repeated pain: may need removal using endoscopic methods through the natural urinary passage
  • Large kidney stones: may need a keyhole procedure through a small cut in the back

Your surgeon will recommend the method after seeing your reports, and will explain the benefits and risks in simple language.

Preventing kidney stones

Simple habits make a big difference:

  • Drink enough water through the day so that urine stays pale
  • Reduce salt and limit processed food
  • Do not overdo animal protein
  • Eat fruits and vegetables, and keep a normal amount of calcium in your diet
  • Follow your doctor's advice about foods that are high in oxalate if you have had stones before

When to seek urgent help

Go to a hospital without delay if you have unbearable pain, fever with chills, repeated vomiting, or if you are unable to pass urine. A blocked and infected kidney is an emergency and needs prompt treatment.

Conclusion
