Laser Piles Treatment: How It Works and What Recovery Looks Like

Understand how laser treatment shrinks piles, who it suits, what the procedure feels like and how quickly you can resume routine.

News12 Nov 2026By Dr. Abhijeet Suresh Shinde5 min read

Understand how laser treatment shrinks piles, who it suits, what the procedure feels like and how quickly you can resume routine.

Piles, also called haemorrhoids, are swollen blood vessels in the anal canal that cause bleeding, itching, pain or a lump. Laser treatment is a modern, minimally invasive option that many patients prefer to traditional surgery. This article explains how it works and what to expect.

What are piles?

Piles form when the cushions of tissue and blood vessels in the anal canal swell because of pressure. Constipation, straining, long hours of sitting, low-fibre diet, pregnancy and a family history are common causes. Piles are graded from one to four depending on how far they come out, and the grade guides the treatment.

How laser treatment works

In laser treatment, a thin laser fibre delivers controlled energy directly to the pile tissue. The energy shrinks the swollen tissue and seals the feeding blood vessels, so the pile reduces in size and the bleeding settles. Because the laser acts precisely on the affected area, the surrounding healthy tissue is largely protected.

Who may be suitable?

Laser treatment is commonly considered for patients with bleeding or protruding piles who have not improved with medicines and diet changes. Suitability depends on the grade, size and associated problems such as a fissure or fistula, so a proper examination comes first. Very large or complicated piles may need a different surgical approach, and your surgeon will tell you honestly which option fits you.

Possible advantages

Compared with conventional surgery, laser treatment is generally associated with:

  • Less pain and bleeding during and after the procedure
  • A small, precise treatment with minimal tissue damage
  • Day-care treatment in most cases
  • A quicker return to routine activities

What happens during the procedure

The procedure is usually done under anaesthesia chosen by the team for your comfort, and it takes a relatively short time. After an examination of the anal canal, the laser is applied to the affected piles. You are observed for a short while and, in most cases, go home the same day with medicines and clear instructions.

Recovery and aftercare

Mild discomfort or a small amount of discharge in the first few days is common. Most people return to light work within a few days, although the time varies from person to person. To support healing:

  • Eat high-fibre food and drink plenty of water
  • Avoid straining and do not sit on the toilet for long
  • Take warm sitz baths as advised
  • Take the prescribed medicines and stool softeners regularly
  • Attend the follow-up visit

Will piles come back?

Treatment removes the existing piles, but the habits that caused them can bring them back. Keeping stools soft, staying active and avoiding long periods of straining are the best ways to prevent recurrence. No treatment can promise that piles will never return, and a good surgeon will tell you this clearly.

When to see a doctor

Do not assume that all anal bleeding is piles. Consult a surgeon if you see blood in stools, have persistent pain, notice a change in bowel habits or have bleeding along with weight loss, so that more serious conditions are not missed.
