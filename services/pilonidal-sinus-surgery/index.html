Overview

What Is a Pilonidal Sinus?

A pilonidal sinus is a small channel or pit in the skin at the top of the buttock cleft. It can cause pain, swelling and discharge, and sometimes forms an abscess.

Treatment ranges from abscess drainage to minimally invasive pit-picking, sinus excision and flap procedures that lower recurrence. Wound care and hair control are an important part of cure.

Pilonidal abscess

A painful, swollen collection of pus. Needs drainage.

Chronic sinus

Repeated discharge from one or more openings at the cleft. Needs definitive treatment.

Symptoms

Symptoms of Pilonidal Sinus: When to See a Specialist

Common symptoms

  • Pain or tenderness at the top of the buttock cleft
  • A swelling or lump that may become red and hot
  • Discharge of pus or blood-stained fluid
  • Small pits or openings in the skin
  • Fever and feeling unwell when an abscess forms

See a specialist if

  • A painful swelling in the cleft
  • Discharge continues or comes and goes
  • It keeps returning after drainage
  • Pain makes sitting hard
  • You have fever with a red, swollen area

Is it something else?

  • It can look like a simple boil
  • Repeated drainage without definitive care often fails
  • A short examination confirms it
  • Early treatment keeps surgery smaller

A hot, rapidly swelling, very painful lump with fever needs urgent attention, as an abscess usually needs drainage.

Causes & risk factors

What Causes Pilonidal Sinus?

The exact cause is a mix of hair, friction and skin changes in the cleft. Loose hairs can burrow into the skin and trigger infection.

It is common in young adults, especially in those who sit for long hours or have thick body hair.

Non-Surgical Care for Pilonidal Sinus in Kharadi, Pune

Comfort measures

  • Warm baths for comfort

Minimal procedures

  • Quick, often day-care

Everyday habits

Healthy Habits That Help With Pilonidal Sinus

Small daily changes make a big difference to treatment, and they also lower the chance of pilonidal sinus returning.

Helps recovery

  • Protein: dal, eggs, curd, paneer
  • Fresh fruit and vegetables
  • Plenty of water
  • Whole grains

Habits to change

  • Long hours of unbroken sitting
  • Tight clothing in the cleft
  • Ignoring a small swelling
  • Skipping dressing visits

Good wound care and hair control matter as much as the operation.

Minimally Invasive Options for Pilonidal Sinus

Pit-picking and similar techniques treat the sinus through small openings, so wounds are smaller and healing is quicker.

These suit selected patients, and Dr. Shinde explains honestly when a larger repair is the safer choice.

Who may be a good candidate?

  • A wish for smaller wounds
  • No active abscess
  • Fit for a day-care procedure

Honest note

  • Recurrence is still possible
  • Hair control remains important
  • Some patients need a flap repair
