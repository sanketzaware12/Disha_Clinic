Overview

What Are Gallstones?

Gallstones are hardened deposits that form in the gallbladder. They can cause sharp pain in the upper right abdomen, nausea and indigestion, especially after fatty meals.

When symptoms keep returning or complications develop, removing the gallbladder by laparoscopic cholecystectomy is the most reliable solution. You can live normally without a gallbladder.

Silent stones

Stones that cause no symptoms. Often found by chance and watched.

Symptomatic stones

Stones causing pain, infection or other complications. Surgery is usually advised.

Types and options

Main Types and Approaches in Gallstone Disease

Gallstone Disease is not the same for every patient. Knowing which approach suits you helps us plan treatment properly.

Cholesterol stones

The most common type, linked to diet and weight.

Pigment stones

Darker stones, linked to blood and liver conditions.

Gallbladder inflammation

Infection or swelling of the gallbladder with fever and pain.

Bile duct stones

Stones that move into the bile duct and may block it.

Symptoms

Symptoms of Gallstone Disease: When to See a Specialist

Common symptoms

  • Pain in the upper right abdomen or centre, often after fatty food
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Bloating and indigestion
  • Pain spreading to the back or right shoulder
  • Pain that comes in attacks

See a specialist if

  • Repeated pain attacks
  • Fever with abdominal pain
  • Yellow eyes or skin
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Severe pain that does not settle

Is it something else?

  • Acidity and ulcers can feel similar
  • Pain can come from the liver or pancreas
  • Ultrasound confirms stones quickly
  • Early care avoids emergencies

Severe pain with fever, yellow eyes or repeated vomiting can mean a complication such as infection or a blocked duct and needs urgent care.

Causes & risk factors

What Causes Gallstone Disease?

Gallstones form when bile contains too much cholesterol or pigment, or when the gallbladder does not empty well.

They are more common in women, with age, in pregnancy and with rapid weight change.

How Is Gallstone Disease Diagnosed?

Diagnosis is simple, quick and done in privacy. You are told what is being done at each step.

  2. 2

  4. 4

    Blood tests

    Liver and infection markers; extra scans only if needed.

  • Gradual weight loss only

Medicines

Watching silent stones

Everyday habits

Healthy Habits That Help With Gallstone Disease

Small daily changes make a big difference to treatment, and they also lower the chance of gallstone disease returning.

Helps

  • Fruit, vegetables and whole grains
  • Light, low-fat home-cooked food
  • Small, regular meals
  • Plenty of water

Go easy on

  • Fried and oily food
  • Heavy, fatty meals
  • Long fasting gaps
  • Crash diets

Introduce changes gradually; very fast weight loss can form stones.

What Can Happen If Gallstone Disease Is Ignored?

Silent stones can be watched, but stones that cause pain rarely settle. Waiting can lead to complications.

Repeated attacks

Pain returns again and again.

Infection

Gallbladder inflammation with fever.

Blocked bile duct

Yellow eyes and liver problems.

Pancreatitis

A serious inflammation needing hospital care.

Myths and facts

Common Myths About Gallstone Disease

Myth“Stones can be dissolved or flushed out.”
Fact

Home or herbal ‘flushes’ do not remove stones and can be harmful.

Myth“Gallbladder removal ruins digestion.”
Fact

Most people eat a nearly normal diet after a short adjustment.

Myth“Only overweight people get stones.”
Fact

Many types of people can form stones.

Myth“Surgery is very risky.”
Fact

Keyhole surgery is common and safe in experienced hands.
